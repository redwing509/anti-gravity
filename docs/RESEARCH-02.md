## 1. What the opened literature actually says

### The force in air is real, and it is ionic wind

Opened official sources agree that an asymmetric high-voltage electrode
pair in air produces a measurable force, and that the force is the
momentum given to the surrounding air (ionic or corona wind), not a
coupling of electromagnetism to gravity.

Cabanas et al. 2022, University of Oviedo, official MDPI text:

> "Subsequent studies confirmed that the gravitational field was
> unaffected, and that the thrust was produced by a phenomenon now
> known as ionic wind."

Wilson, Perkins, and Thompson, NASA/TM—2009-215822, reviewing Tajmar's
sealed-box test and then reporting their own quantitative checks:

> "In an elegant experiment, Tajmar enclosed a corona discharge entirely
> inside a box suspended on wires. No motion of the box was observed
> when the discharge was turned on, even though a very sensitive motion
> detection system was used. Since the enclosure blocked the ionic wind,
> but should have no effect on other mechanisms, this indicates that the
> ionic wind generates the thrust."

They then measured the electric lift against the ionic-wind momentum
directly. On a hexagonal lifter (six 150 mm sides, 0.07 mm emitter wires
32 mm above foil-wrapped collectors, total weight 9 g, lift-off at
24.6 kV and 1.4 mA), a shield between the lifter and the balance blocked
the wind from hitting the pan. They report:

> "That, to the accuracy of this experiment, the balance reading did not
> change as current increased when the hexalifter was mounted directly on
> the balance, shows that the electric lift equals the thrust from the
> ionic wind, i.e., that Te = Tiw."

And, with a thrust plate:

> "As can be seen from Figure 3.4, except at the upper values of current,
> the values of Te from the balance, and Tiw from the thrust plate are in
> very good agreement."

The report abstract states the same conclusion without the figure:

> "An initial experiment showed that the thrust observed did equal the
> thrust of the ionic wind."

Tajmar's own opened TU Dresden PDF describes the sealed Faraday-box
design (50 cm wooden cube, exterior grounded aluminum foil; aluminum
cylinder and ring electrodes, 6 cm separation, up to about 40 kV to
avoid sparks) and the force bound. Positioning accuracy gave a force
accuracy of 10.5 µN. After raising the supply to about 38 kV:

> "If a linear thrust exists in the presented configuration, the data
> suggests that it must be below 10 µN."

He compares that bound to a calculated corona-wind thrust of 163 mN:

> "Comparing this value to our obtained upper limit for an additional
> effect of 10 µN, a possible gravitation-electromagnetism interaction
> must be at least 5 orders of magnitude below the electric wind
> effects."

The opened numbers in that same passage are 163 mN and 10 µN. Their
ratio is 1.63 × 10⁴, or about 4.2 orders of magnitude, not 5. The
quotations are kept as written. The "5 orders" wording is the paper's
own coarser statement, not a second measurement.

The abstract states the propulsion implication and the interpretation
(the typographical error "ration" is in the opened PDF and is left as
written):

> "Measurements indicate, that such anomalous force must be at least
> five orders of magnitude below corona wind phenomena and have at least
> a two orders of magnitude higher power-to-thrust ration compared to
> traditional electric propulsion thrusters. Hence, even if the effect
> exists, it would be not attractive for space propulsion. The obtained
> results suggest that corona wind effects were misinterpreted as a
> connection between gravity and electromagnetism."

At the 38 kV, 0.6 mA point he writes the power-to-thrust bound as
P ≥ 2280 W/mN, from 38 kV × 0.6 mA = 22.8 W and the 10 µN ceiling.

### Vacuum and reduced-pressure tests do not support a gravity-linked force

Opened official NASA reports find no usable force once there is no
air, or once the pressure is too low to support the discharge in the
usual way.

Canning, Melcher, and Winet, NASA/CR—2004-213312:

> "Our data did not show any evidence for forces in a vacuum."

On rotation tests at reduced pressure:

> "After several days of tests, we found that no device showed signs of
> rotation at a pressure less than 300 Torr, with one exception."

The exception was at 5.5 × 10⁻⁵ Torr and 44 kV, where "a large arc was
observed" and the device "was seen to move about an eighth of a
rotation and stop." The opened report attributes that motion to ejected
material, not to a residual gravity-like force:

> "The large arc that was observed suggests that this movement was most
> likely caused by material being ejected from the device."

The companion ISR conference paper (opened NTRS PDF) states, in its
abstract:

> "While no force was measured in a vacuum, some suggested design
> changes are given for operation in reduced pressures."

Tajmar, reviewing Brown's vacuum claims against later work, is explicit
that the vacuum-thrust claim is a patent claim, not a scientific-paper
result:

> "This thrust was claimed to be not due to corona wind phenomena and
> also exists in vacuum. These claims, although only published in
> patents, survived until recent publications for very advanced
> propulsion concepts."

On Talley, Tajmar (opened TU Dresden PDF) writes:

> "Two decades later, Talley studied Brown’s electrode configurations in
> vacuum chambers up to 10-6 Torr in great detail19,20."

The "10-6" in that sentence is the extraction of a superscript in the
opened PDF (10⁻⁶ Torr). Tajmar then writes that Talley found "no thrust
in the case of a static DC potential applied to the electrodes. However
he noticed an anomalous force during electrical breakdowns when a
current was flowing."

Canning, Melcher, and Winet quote Talley's own wording in the opened
NASA contractor report (the official DTIC full PDF was not opened this
pass; the quotation below is NASA's quotation of Talley, including the
ellipses in the NASA text):

> "Direct experimental results show that under high vacuum
> conditions… no detectable propulsive force was electrostatically
> induced by applying a static potential difference… between test
> device electrodes…"

And, still in NASA/CR—2004-213312, Talley's conclusion as quoted from
page 91 of his report:

> "If such a force still exists and lies below the threshold of
> measurements in this program, then the force may be too small to be
> attractive for many, if not most, space propulsion applications."

Canning et al. also write that Talley "ultimately attributed the force
that he observed to the electrostatic interaction between the chamber
and the device."

Nothing in the official texts opened this pass shows a replicated
Biefeld-Brown force in high vacuum that would require a new
gravitational coupling.

### The 2003 "ion wind is too small" disagreement

RESEARCH-01 left open a disagreement between Bahder and Fazi (2003) and
later ionic-wind literature. The opened 2003 arXiv / ARL text says, in
the abstract:

> "The calculations indicate that ionic wind is at least three orders of
> magnitude too small to explain the magnitude of the observed force on
> the capacitor. The ionic drift transport assumption leads to the
> correct order of magnitude for the force, however, it is difficult to
> see how ionic drift enters into the theory."

They had verified a net force on four capacitor shapes, at about 30 kV,
toward the smaller electrode, and they stated that "At present, the
physical basis for the Biefeld-Brown effect is not understood." They
asked for vacuum tests and a plasma / charge-transport model. They did
not claim a gravitational mechanism.

Later opened work answers the vacuum request and gives measurements
that a charge-transport or drift model would have to reproduce. The
vacuum tests above are nulls for a residual force. Wilson's balance and
thrust-plate comparison shows Te = Tiw to the accuracy of that
experiment. Tajmar's sealed box removes the wind and removes the net
force. Cabanas et al. treat the effect as ionic wind and measure it as
such. None of those later official texts says that it is a reply to
Bahder and Fazi.

The 2003 "three orders too small" bound was a simple ballistic-ion-wind
estimate: in Bahder and Fazi's wording, "ionic wind" there is the
momentum of the ions themselves. In the same paper, the ionic-drift
estimate already gave the right order of magnitude. The later official
measurements use "ionic wind" for the air flow driven by ion drift and
collisions — ordinary EHD — and identify that air momentum as the force
that is actually measured in air. Those are two uses of the same words,
not two measurements of one ion-only force. This register's own
conclusion is that the later quantitative tests account for the
atmospheric force, and that the 2003 ballistic bound is not a remaining
anomaly.

### Documented atmospheric geometries and numbers

Values below are only from texts opened this pass, in the units those
texts use.

**Cabanas et al. 2022** (University of Oviedo; official MDPI page).
Emitter: bare copper wire, 0.25 mm diameter. Collector: hollow aluminum
tube, 6 mm outer diameter, 5 mm inner diameter. Power supply: Baker
D30R, results at 30 kV unless a table row says otherwise. Thrust
balance accuracy 0.01 g.

- 100 mm electrodes: maximum thrust 0.6 g.
- 400 mm single electrodes: maximum thrust 3.2 g. Current rose from
  190 µA to 400 µA. "The distances between electrodes that produce the
  maximum thrust range between 25 and 40 mm."
- 400 mm dual electrodes: "The maximum thrust is now of 6.4 g, twice
  that obtained with the previous configuration"
- Dual 400 mm energy-efficiency table (Table 2, g/W): a high listed
  value is 29.4 g/W at 21 kV and 40 mm electrode spacing. The paper
  compares "30 g/W" for EHD with a laboratory motor-propeller
  measurement of 4.3 g/W overall, and writes that EHD "is almost 10
  times more efficient, in terms of energy consumption, than
  conventional electric propulsion" in that comparison. Those g/W
  figures are gram-force per watt as used in that paper. In the same
  discussion the authors also write: "Its application to atmospheric
  flight is still far from being an alternative, although in this
  study it has been analyzed, to the best of the authors’ knowledge, in
  a different way to that presented to date."

**Wilson, Perkins, and Thompson, NASA/TM—2009-215822.** Hexagonal
lifter geometry as given above (9 g, 24.6 kV, 1.4 mA, 32 mm gap). A
second "box lifter" used 13 parallel ground electrodes, each 150 mm
long, 4.5 mm leading-edge radius, in a 150 mm × 150 mm × 50 mm
styrofoam duct, with 0.075 mm copper emitters 32 mm above the ground
plane, powered by a Glassman EQ30P40 supply (30 kV, 40 mA). On scaling
for aircraft propulsion, the report abstract says:

> "Parametric experiments, and theory, showed that the thrust per unit
> power could be raised from early values of 5 N/kW to values
> approaching 50 N/kW, but only by lowering the thrust produced, and
> raising the voltage applied. [...] It was concluded that the use of a
> corona discharge for aircraft propulsion did not seem very practical."

Cabanas's "30 g/W" (gram-force per watt) converts, if 1 gram-force is
taken as 9.81 mN, to about 294 N/kW. That is higher than Wilson's
"values approaching 50 N/kW." The two official figures are left in
their own units and are not forced into agreement here. A separate
gap sits inside Cabanas et al. themselves. In the same paragraph that
reports 3.2 g and a 400 µA current for the 400 mm single thruster, a
naive 30 kV × 400 µA = 12 W and 3.2 g / 12 W is about 0.27 g/W, two
orders of magnitude below the 29.4 g/W Table 2 entry at 21 kV and
40 mm. The paper does not, in the opened text, give a current for that
specific Table 2 cell, so this 0.27 g/W figure is this register's
arithmetic from the 3.2 g / 400 µA / 30 kV numbers, not a number
printed as such. Before any later model is checked against 29.4 g/W or
30 g/W, the Table 2 units and the current that belongs to that cell
should be confirmed. A model, when one is approved, should compare to
each source in that source's geometry and units.

**Xu et al., Nature, PubMed abstract only.** Official abstract, not the
paywalled full text:

> "We flew a fixed-wing aeroplane with a five-metre wingspan ten times
> and showed that it achieved steady-level flight. All batteries and
> power systems, including a specifically developed ultralight
> high-voltage (40-kilovolt) power converter, were carried on-board."

The opened abstract does not state the aircraft mass. A 2.4 kg figure
appears on the official ANR project page quoted in the France section
below; that figure is ANR's wording, not a number read from the Nature
paper this pass.

**Tajmar sealed-box geometry**, already given: aluminum cylinder and
ring, 6 cm gap, box 50 cm × 50 cm × 50 cm, ~38 kV, up to 0.6 mA, linear
thrust bound < 10 µN, force accuracy 10.5 µN.

### Germany / German-language institutional record

A later official German-language record at TU Dresden describes

from the official German chair page opened this pass:

> "Ion-Wind-Thruster basieren auf dem Prinzip der Elektrohydrodynamik,
> bei dem ein elektrisches Feld einen Ionenstrom in einem fluiden
> Medium, typischerweise Luft, erzeugt. Dabei ionisiert ein
> Hochspannungsfeld an einer Emissionselektrode die umgebenden
> Luftmoleküle. Die entstandenen geladenen Teilchen werden zu einer
> Sammel-Elektrode entgegengesetzter Polarität beschleunigt, wodurch
> sie Bewegungsenergie auf neutrale Luftmoleküle übertragen. Dieser
> Impulstransfer erzeugt eine Schubkraft, den sogenannten Ionenwind,
> der für Antriebs- oder Strömungssteuerungszwecke ohne bewegliche
> Teile genutzt werden kann – eine effiziente und geräuschlose
> Technologie."

English translation:

> "Ion-wind thrusters are based on the principle of electrohydrodynamics,
> in which an electric field produces a flow of ions in a fluid medium,
> typically air. In this process, a high-voltage field at an emitter
> electrode ionizes the surrounding air molecules. The resulting charged
> particles are accelerated toward a collector electrode of opposite
> polarity, thereby transferring kinetic energy to neutral air
> molecules. This momentum transfer produces a thrust force, the
> so-called ionic wind, which can be used for propulsion or flow-control
> purposes without moving parts — an efficient and silent technology."

The official English version of the same TU Dresden page says
"transferring momentum to neutral air molecules" where the German page
says "Bewegungsenergie" (kinetic energy). The translation above follows
the German wording for that clause and then uses "momentum transfer"
for "Impulstransfer," which is the next sentence in the same paragraph.
The page also states that the chair's vacuum wind tunnel is used to
test these thrusters under low-density atmospheric conditions, which is
not a claim of thrust in a true vacuum.

## 3. Ratings

These ratings are literature conclusions for this class of claim. They
are not model results, and they do not authorize an engine install.

- **Conventional atmospheric EHD / ionic wind: simulate, when James
  approves an engine.** The force is real, the mechanism is documented,
  and opened sources give geometries and numbers that an independent
  fluid / electrostatic solver can be asked to reproduce (Cabanas
  wire-to-tube; Wilson hex and box lifters; Tajmar cylinder-ring as a
  contained-wind null). This remains the first Phase 2 modeling case
  in testability order. Do not install or run a solver until that
  approval is given.
- **Gravity-linked or vacuum Biefeld-Brown force: drop, for this
  class, unless a documented vacuum force appears in an opened official
  source.** Opened official vacuum and sealed-box tests are nulls or
  are accounted for as corona wind, arcs, or chamber electrostatics.
  Brown's vacuum-thrust claims are, in Tajmar's opened wording, patent
  claims. A new official vacuum measurement could reopen this rating;
  none was opened here.
- **Atmospheric EHD as practical large-aircraft propulsion: do not
  promote.** Wilson concluded that corona-discharge propulsion "did not
  seem very practical" for aircraft, even while measuring real thrust
  and higher N/kW at lower thrust. Cabanas argues a g/W advantage over
  a particular motor-propeller set and still calls application to
  atmospheric flight "still far from being an alternative." Those are
  engineering assessments of a conventional force, not evidence of a
  new gravitational effect.
